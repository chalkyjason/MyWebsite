Callsign FPV is the toolkit I wanted at the field: the sounds my radio plays, the font my goggles show, and my own emblem on the start screen, all made on the phone already in my pocket.

### Why this app

EdgeTX radios are picky about audio. A callout only plays if it's a 32 kHz, mono, 16-bit WAV with the exact filename the radio expects for that event, and getting there usually means a desktop audio editor and a filename cheat sheet. Betaflight's analog OSD has the opposite problem: the stock font is thin and disappears against a bright sky or a noisy feed.

### How it works

Pick an audio file from Files, trim it, choose the event it should play on, and Callsign FPV converts it right on the iPhone, using the phone's own audio decoder. Save the result to Files or AirDrop it to your computer and drop it on the SD card. Every clip you convert is kept in My Sounds.

On the OSD side, browse the three Army Jay fonts glyph by glyph and edit any of them pixel by pixel. Then build the start screen Betaflight shows at power-up like a game emblem: stack shapes from skulls and eagles to rank chevrons and quads, add your callsign, and drag, pinch and twist each layer into place. The result is a standard `.mcm` file for Betaflight Configurator's Font Manager.

### Privacy-first by design

No account. No analytics. No crash reports. No network calls. The files you pick are read on your phone, and everything Callsign FPV saves (your converted sounds, font edits and splash designs) stays in the app's storage on your device. Callsign FPV is open source; the code is [on GitHub](https://github.com/chalkyjason/edgesounds).
