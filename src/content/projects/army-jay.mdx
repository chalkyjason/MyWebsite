Army Jay is the toolkit I wanted at the field: the sounds my radio plays and the font my goggles show, both made on the phone already in my pocket.

### Why this app

EdgeTX radios are picky about audio. A callout only plays if it's a 32 kHz, mono, 16-bit WAV with the exact filename the radio expects for that event, and getting there usually means a desktop audio editor and a filename cheat sheet. Betaflight's analog OSD has the opposite problem: the stock font is thin and disappears against a bright sky or a noisy feed.

### How it works

Pick an audio file from Files, trim it, choose the event it should play on, and Army Jay converts it right on the iPhone, using the phone's own audio decoder. Save the result to Files or AirDrop it to your computer and drop it on the SD card. Every clip you convert is kept in My Sounds.

On the OSD side, browse the three Army Jay fonts glyph by glyph, edit any of them pixel by pixel, or design the boot splash Betaflight shows at power-up. The result is a standard `.mcm` file for Betaflight Configurator's Font Manager.

### Privacy-first by design

No account. No analytics. No crash reports. No network calls. The files you pick are read on your phone, and everything Army Jay saves (your converted sounds, font edits and splash designs) stays in the app's storage on your device. Army Jay is open source; the code is [on GitHub](https://github.com/chalkyjason/edgesounds).

### Disclaimer

Army Jay is not affiliated with or endorsed by the EdgeTX or Betaflight projects. Always check your radio's sounds and your OSD on the bench before you fly.
